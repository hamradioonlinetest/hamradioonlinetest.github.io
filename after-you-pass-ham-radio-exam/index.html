What to expect

Results and FCC processing

  • The VE team sends your results and submits your application through ARRL VEC.
  • A new license requires a separate $35 FCC application payment.
  • A normal license-class upgrade is exempt from that FCC application fee.
  • Check the FCC ULS database for the final license grant or updated class.

Processing times vary. Contact us if your application appears delayed or you need help understanding its status.

Next actions

If this is your first license

  1. Save your results email and Certificate of Successful Completion of Examination (CSCE).
  2. Track your application with the WEARC FRN lookup and the FCC ULS application search.
  3. Pay the separate $35 FCC application fee directly to the FCC through CORES. The payment window is 10 calendar days from the date the FCC application file number is issued.
  4. Watch for the FCC payment email, including in spam folders. You do not need to wait for that email if your application is already in the FCC system.
  5. Confirm your callsign and license grant in the FCC ULS database before operating as the control operator of your own station. A CSCE alone does not authorize a first-time applicant to transmit.

See the payment guide and FCC application fee instructions from ARRL. Eligible new-license candidates under 18 can apply for ARRL fee reimbursement after their license is issued.

If you upgraded an existing license

  1. Save the CSCE showing the elements you passed and verify that the VE team has your correct existing callsign and FRN.
  2. Track the application and confirm your new license class in the FCC ULS database.
  3. Do not expect a $35 payment request for an ordinary license-class upgrade. Other application types, such as a vanity callsign request or renewal, have separate fee rules.

Existing licensees who meet 47 CFR § 97.9(b) may use temporary upgraded privileges while their application is pending, until final disposition or 365 days after passing, whichever comes first. When using those privileges, follow the station identification requirements: append /AG for General or /AE for Amateur Extra. Ask the VE team if you are unsure whether you qualify.

Get on the air

Once you have the appropriate operating authority, set up your station and join a WEARC meeting or net for support.

Need help on your first contact? WEARC can help you get on the air with confidence.
Licensed while outside the United States? Your FCC license does not automatically authorize operation from another country. Before transmitting outside areas regulated by the FCC, follow the licensing, permit, and reciprocal-operating requirements of the country where the station is located. See ARRL International Operating.
